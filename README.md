My goal is to extend this foundation into scalable data engineering and AI applications for building performance, fault detection, and energy management.

## Data Engineering Through Building Systems

My controls work provides practical experience with the foundations of operational data engineering: acquiring values, validating them, processing them, and delivering useful outputs.

A representative integration path is:

**Physical meter → Modbus/RS-485 → LoRaWAN transmitter → Gateway → BACnet → Niagara → Trends and dashboards**

At each stage, I consider questions such as:

- Is the device communicating consistently?
- Is the correct register or point being read?
- Does the value represent the intended measurement?
- Are units and scaling correct?
- Is the reading current, stale, missing, or invalid?
- Does the displayed value agree with the physical meter?
- Can downstream calculations handle resets and interruptions?

I am expanding these skills into Python- and SQL-based workflows for collecting, organizing, validating, and analyzing time-series data.

## How I Approach Engineering Problems

1. **Understand the system.**  
   Review the equipment, intended sequence, available points, and operating constraints.

2. **Verify the inputs.**  
   Check communication, mapping, units, timestamps, and agreement with field observations.

3. **Establish a baseline.**  
   Determine expected behavior before introducing more complex calculations or models.

4. **Build and test incrementally.**  
   Validate individual components before relying on the complete workflow.

5. **Document the evidence.**  
   Record what was tested, what passed, what remains uncertain, and what needs attention.

6. **Make the result useful.**  
   Present findings in a form that operators, engineers, and project stakeholders can act on.
